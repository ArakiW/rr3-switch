# THE PROBLEM TO FIX

The port has an autopilot ('the pursuit') that steers the car by aiming at a point on the track's racing line. In the most recent measured round (tag chunk1, 2026-11-10) the pursuit is saturating the steering at full lock while its steering error swings between +1.16 and -2.66 radians, and the car goes BACKWARDS:

    perr= 1.1621  steer=-1.000  total=100657
    perr=-1.6103  steer= 1.000  total=100565
    perr= 2.6651  steer=-1.000  total= 98711
    perr=-0.2034  steer= 0.407  total= 95994
    perr=-2.4641  steer= 1.000  total= 94034

perr is the signed angle from the car's heading to the direction of the aim point. An error of 2.4 rad (137 degrees) means the aim point is BEHIND the car. The controller then applies full lock, the car spins, and the guest's own progress along the track falls.

Three root causes, all in the pursuit path. Fix all three.

# FIX A - THE AIM POINT MUST COME FROM THE PROJECTION, AND ITS LOOK-AHEAD MUST NEVER BE ZERO

Today raceLineTargetAdaptive() finds the nearest NODE of the polyline, then walks `lookAhead` metres along
the locked direction. When the car is more than kOnLineDistanceMetres (25 m) from the line, the look-ahead is
forced to 0.0, so the aim point IS that node. The nodes are about 8 m apart, so that node can be up to about
4 m BEHIND the car along the track, and at a look-ahead of zero the vector from the car to the aim point is
decided by which side the node happened to fall on. That is the geometry that produced the 2.4 rad error.

Required:

A1. Compute the car's PROJECTION onto the polyline: the nearest point on the nearest SEGMENT (not the nearest
    node), plus the ARC-LENGTH COORDINATE of that projection, measured in metres along the polyline in NODE
    ORDER, starting at node 0, wrapping at the loop length. The coordinate is a property of the LINE, so it
    does not change when the travel direction flips; the caller compares two coordinates and must be
    wrap-around aware.

A2. The aim point is the point at (projection arc coordinate + lookAhead) along the locked direction. When the
    car is far from the line, the look-ahead is a fixed moderate value (a point on the line ahead of the car),
    NOT zero. Keep kFarFromLineLimitMetres as the hard limit and keep kOnLineDistanceMetres as the on-line
    threshold, and keep the curvature-following look-ahead for a car that is on the line.

A3. Expose the projection and the arc-length coordinate through the public API so the caller can use the same
    number for FIX B and FIX C. Add it to E:\AI\RealRacing3-switch\native-switch\include\rr3\race_line.hpp.

A4. A car that is ON the line and driving FORWARD must get an aim point ahead of it, so the error stays small.
    A car that is ON the line and driving BACKWARD must still get an aim point that is AHEAD ALONG THE TRACK
    (that is the honest report of 'you are going the wrong way round'), and the caller sees a large error.

# FIX B - THE FLIP CRITERION MUST NOT BE A SIGNAL THE RECOVERY CAN FAKE

Today the flip fires on `rate`, a 120 frame smoothed rate, and the direction-lock inversion right below it is
dead code (`if (false && ...)`). The reason it is dead is written in the comment: the recovery deliberately
REVERSES the car, so the recovery's own reverse drives the rate negative, and since the recovery lasts 240
frames (kDriveRecoveryFrames) while the rate window is 120 frames, the flip fires on the recovery's signal.
The comment there already states the requirement:

    TO RESTORE IT, the criterion has to be the car's own progress along the track (the guest's signed total),
    not a smoothed rate that the recovery can poison.

Required:

B1. Compute the flip criterion from the ALONG-TRACK COORDINATE from FIX A1, compared over a window LONGER than
    kDriveRecoveryFrames, so the recovery's own 240 frame reverse cannot dominate the window. The window must be
    a named constant with the reasoning in a comment. The comparison must be wrap-around aware (the coordinate
    wraps at the loop length).

B2. Keep the steering-sign flip: it is what stops the controller driving the wrong way. Base it on the windowed
    along-track comparison instead of the smoothed rate, and keep the existing 600 frame hysteresis between
    flips.

B3. RESTORE the direction-lock inversion, driven by the SAME along-track criterion, not by the instantaneous
    rate. The lock is the port's one-off decision about which way round the track is; it has to be invertible
    when the measurement proves the car is going the other way. Removing the `if (false && ...)` is part of the
    job.

B4. The recovery itself must not be able to trigger the flip. Keep the existing `!g_drive.brake` guard, and
    make sure the window length and the hysteresis are consistent with kDriveRecoveryFrames.

# FIX C - THE STUCK TEST MUST MEASURE PROGRESS ALONG THE TRACK, NOT THE WORLD POSITION

Today the pinned test watches the car's world position and resets its timer whenever the car has moved

pin.

Required:
