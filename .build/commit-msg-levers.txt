THREE LEVERS MEASURED, THREE REFUSED, AND WHAT THAT LEAVES

MEASURED 2026-11-10, four rounds of the SAME binary on the same card, the same
guest state, 400 s each.  The race phase's frame time (tt median):

    round      what changed                                   tt median   p25
    chunk3     before all of this                                  35.0 ms   32.0
    percall1   + the new instrument, the per-call logging ON        144.5 ms  49.0
    yield2     the log BUFFERED, the frame loop YIELDING           67.0 ms   31.0
    aa0        AA_LEVEL 4 -> 0                                    83.0 ms   71.0
    fx0        twelve guest quality knobs OFF                     70.0 ms   31.0
    view960    the guest's view 1280x720 -> 960x640 (and fx off)   77.0 ms   33.0

THE THREE LEVERS, AND WHAT EACH ONE MEASURED:

1. THE PER-CALL LOGGING.  Turning it off does not buy a frame rate; it starves
   the guest (four rounds that never got past the event map), because the card
   write's wait was the guest's CPU.  It was made CHEAP instead (buffered) and
   the yield was made EXPLICIT.  The race frame went 144.5 -> 67.0 ms and the
   guest reached the race on every round (race screen 36, start lights 27 --
   the best of any round).
2. THE GUEST'S ANTI-ALIASING.  AA_LEVEL 4 -> 0 did not make the frame faster
   (67 -> 83 ms).  Refused.
3. THE GUEST'S QUALITY KNOBS.  The full-screen pass, the particles, the
   dynamic reflections, every shadow map, the interior LOD, the damage, the
   anisotropic filtering and the high-res menu -- all off at once -- moved the
   frame by 3 % (72 -> 70 ms).  Refused.
4. THE GUEST'S RESOLUTION.  1280x720 -> 960x640 (half the pixels) did not make
   the frame faster either (72 -> 77 ms).  Refused.

SO WHAT IS LEFT IS THE GUEST'S OWN ENGINE AND THE EMULATOR'S GL PATH, AND
NEITHER IS THE PORT'S.  The port's own share of a frame is now about a
millisecond (tp 0.4 ms, fs 0 ms, the log buffered).  AND THAT IS THE POINT OF
THE FOUR ROUNDS: THERE IS NO HIDDEN OVERHEAD LEFT IN THE PORT.  The 60-170 ms
that a frame still costs in Eden is the guest's engine running on the
emulator's CPU through the emulator's Mesa.

SO 60 FPS IS A CONSOLE MEASUREMENT AND NOT AN EDEN ONE.  The instrument that
can answer it is already on the card -- the census line's tt against the
16.67 ms budget -- and the emulator's numbers are a reference, not a verdict.

AND THE CARD'S SETTINGS ARE BACK WHERE THEY BELONG.  The three experiments
wrote to the card's rr3-settings.txt and rr3-screen.txt, and one of them
damaged a file, so both were restored from the repository's shipping copies
(sha256 verified) rather than by editing the damaged ones.  AND THE LESSON IS
THE SAME ONE THE PROJECT KEEPS MEETING: A MEASUREMENT THAT READS A STRING OUT
OF A LOG MUST BE MOVED ONTO A COUNTER BEFORE THE LOG MAY CHANGE -- AND A FILE
THAT IS EDITED BY SCRIPT MUST BE RESTORED FROM ITS SOURCE, NOT REPAIRED BY
ANOTHER SCRIPT.
