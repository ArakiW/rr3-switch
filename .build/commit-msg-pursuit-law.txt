THE PURSUIT GETS THE GEOMETRY RIGHT, AND THE RECOVERY GETS THE WHEEL

MEASURED 2026-11-10 (round chunk2), the causal chain, every link read out of
the log:

    cchunk = 2.5 to 9.3 m   the chunk picker works
    dir    = 1, mot = (-1.000, -0.007)
    perr   = 1.5 to 2.9 rad (86 to 166 degrees), and it STAYS there
    ctdist = 17 to 25 m     the aim point is the 20 m look-ahead away
    total  = 71,949 -> 58,507 -> 30,865   the car drives BACKWARDS
    brk    = 1, steer = -1.000   the recovery brakes AT FULL LOCK

An error of 180 degrees with the target 20 m away is not geometry: the aim
point is BEHIND the car.  And the port could not escape it:

  * the pursuit OVERRIDES the recovery's steering ("BUT THE PURSUIT WINS"),
    so the recovery's brake plus the pursuit's full lock is a spin;
  * the steering-sign flip is INERT while the pursuit is on, because `steer`
    is `steersign * perr / steergain` and `g_drive.sign` never reaches the
    wheel -- the `flips` counter counted events that did nothing;
  * the one mechanism that could invert an aim point that is behind the car
    was disabled in the source (`if (false && ...)`), and the criterion that
    used to drive it -- a 120 frame smoothed rate -- is exactly the signal
    the recovery poisons, because the recovery reverses the car on purpose.

THE FIVE CHANGES, all in the pursuit's own path:

A. THE AIM POINT COMES FROM THE PROJECTION, NOT THE NEAREST NODE, AND ITS
   LOOK-AHEAD IS NEVER ZERO.  The polyline's nodes are ~8 m apart, so the
   nearest node can be ~4 m BEHIND the car, and the old code forced the
   look-ahead to 0 when the car was more than 25 m off the line -- which
   aimed at the car's own feet.  Now the aim point is the projection on the
   nearest SEGMENT, plus the look-ahead along the locked direction, and the
   off-line look-ahead is a real 10 m.

B. THE FLIP CRITERION IS THE CAR'S OWN PROGRESS ALONG THE TRACK, over a
   window LONGER than the recovery (360 frames against 240), so the
   recovery's own reverse cannot fake a wrong-way reading.  The direction-lock
   inversion is RESTORED on that criterion.

C. THE STUCK TEST MEASURES PROGRESS ALONG THE TRACK, not the car's world
   position, and it ignores the frames the recovery is active.  The old test
   reset its timer on any 1 m of world movement, so a car grinding along a
   barrier never read as stuck and the recovery's reverse hid the pin.

D. INSTRUMENTATION: the race-state line gains `along` (the arc-length
   coordinate), `pdist` (the distance to the line) and `fdelta` (the windowed
   along-track delta the flip uses), and two event lines, `race-flip` and
   `race-stuck`, each with a hard budget of 16 lines and a "budget exhausted"
   line.  The argument order matches the format string: the previous commit
   put a new argument in the wrong place, the compiler warned, nobody read
   the warning, and four fields were printed from the wrong arguments for a
   whole round.

H. THE PURSUIT YIELDS TO THE RECOVERY.  While the recovery is active it owns
   the wheel, so a brake and a full lock can no longer happen at the same
   time; and a pursuit that sits at a steady 80+ degree error without
   advancing along the track is declared a limit cycle and handed to the
   recovery.  The steering's owner is in the log: `owner=pursuit|recovery|
   sweep`.

THE HOST SELF-TEST GAINS FOUR CASES WITH TEETH: a car on the line driving
forward gets an aim point ahead of it and an error under 0.3 rad; a car
driving backwards gets an aim point ahead ALONG THE TRACK and a large error
(the geometry reports the wrong way round instead of aiming behind); a car
30 m off the line gets a non-zero look-ahead and a target at least that far
away; and the along-track coordinate increases forward and decreases
backward.

GATE: 40 run, 0 failed, GATE OK.  Build clean, no -Wformat= warning.

NOTE THE FRAME RATE: the port's measured frame time is 30.35 ms, not 60 fps,
so the 360-frame window is 10.9 s and the 600-frame stuck window is 18.2 s.
