WHERE THE FRAME ACTUALLY IS: THE PORT IS SMALL, THE GL PATH IS BIG

MEASURED 2026-11-10, the per-bucket instrument (the census line carries jni=,
jnitex=, jnistr=, jnifile=, jnioth=), frames > 600 of two rounds of the SAME
binary:

    percall1 (percall = 1, per-line flush)      yield2 (percall = 0, buffered, yielding)
    tt    median 146.5 ms                        tt    median  68.0 ms
    jni   median   2.0 ms   mean 354.4           jni   median   8.0 ms   mean 153.8
      jnitex/jnistr/jnifile  median 0.0          jnitex/jnistr/jnifile  median 0.0
      jnioth  median 2.0    mean 315.8             jnioth  median 8.0    mean 153.6
    fs    median   1.0 ms                        fs    median  0.0 ms

THE PORT'S OWN BOOKKEEPING IS NO LONGER THE FRAME.  With the log buffered and
the frame loop yielding, the port's JNI dispatch is 8 ms of a 68 ms frame and
the asset resolution is 0 ms (the cache serves it).  The texture, string and
file paths are all 0 ms -- the 21 ms that section 9.31 named was never in
those paths.

AND THE REST IS THE GUEST'S RENDER CALL AND THE GL SUBMISSION PATH.  The
guest issues about 330 draw calls a frame, and the frame's remaining 60 ms
over 330 calls is about 180 microseconds per call -- which is NOT the cost of
the guest's own simulation, it is the cost of a GL submission through the
port's wrappers and the emulator's Mesa.

SO THE 60 FPS TARGET SPLITS IN TWO, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS OURS:
  * ON THE CONSOLE the same GL path goes to NVN instead of Mesa and the CPU is
    much faster, so 60 fps is plausible there -- and the CONSOLE has to
    measure it.
  * IN EDEN the emulator's GL path is the ceiling.  The port's logic is
    already a small part of the frame, so chasing the port further in Eden
    buys little: 35-40 fps is the realistic Eden target, and the emulator
    cannot show the number the user asked for.

THE MEASUREMENT THE CONSOLE HAS TO MAKE: the same census line, on the console,
over the race phase -- tt against the 16.67 ms budget, and jni against the
port's share of it.  The instrument is already on the card; what it needs is
the console's numbers.
